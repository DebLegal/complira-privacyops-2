# Complyra PrivacyOps — MVP Product Blueprint
**Version:** 1.0 | **Prepared:** 14 November 2026 | **Market:** India | **Status:** Proposed plan, not a security certification or legal opinion

## Executive decision
Build a **DPDP Processing & Vendor Change-Control Desk**, not a Consent Manager, AI lawyer, free checklist, or all-in-one enterprise privacy platform. The single end-to-end job: **when a company introduces or changes a product feature, data field, processing purpose, vendor or contract, identify what processing records, notices, contractual arrangements, ownership and remediation need review; preserve the evidence and close the work.**

**ICP:** India-focused B2B SaaS firms selling to enterprises, approximately 15–150 employees, a named operations or security owner, 5–30 relevant vendors, published policy, and a live trigger (enterprise questionnaire, major vendor onboarding, investor due diligence, impending May 2027 obligations). These are *prospecting hypotheses*, not a measured market segment. Exclude active breaches, highly regulated BFSI/health overlays, litigation, and demand for statutory certification unless specialist-led.

**Product promise:** "One current record connecting what personal data you process, where it moves, who processes it, what your documents say, what changed and what remains to be fixed." Do not claim that software alone makes users DPDP compliant.

### Where source material must be refined before software use
1. The files recommend **not building custom software before five paid deliveries**, and a client portal only after approximately ten sprints. This blueprint **does not overturn that commercial advice**: build a synthetic internal demonstration now; unlock real customer portals only after demand and security proof.
2. `[V]`, `[A]`, `[M]` tags are source-local confidence labels, **not** a guarantee of legal currency. Store exact statutory text, effective dates and reviewer decisions as versioned records.
3. A **valid Data Processor contract** is explicit in Act s.8(2), not a mandatory separate document titled "DPA" in every case. Avoid treating every missing standalone DPA as a conclusive breach.
4. A formal statutory RoPA obligation for **every** Data Fiduciary is not established by these cited DPDP provisions; present a processing register as a defensible operational practice.
5. Mandatory DPO appointment, independent auditor and DPIA apply to **notified Significant Data Fiduciaries**, not automatically every SaaS startup.
6. For the breach workflow, principal intimation is 'without delay'; the Board gets initial intimation 'without delay' and a further specified update within 72 hours, subject to permitted extension. Do not represent 72 hours as the universal deadline for both.
7. Breach/health/BFSI needs a specialist. A consumer-facing rights portal with real data would create substantially more operational security risk than a metadata-only register.
8. The examples of breach notification to suppliers within 12 hours, rights fulfilment within 15 days, etc. must be tagged `commercial_negotiation`, never `statutory_deadline`.

| One-off notice/policy drafting | High pre-deadline | Low–medium | High | Low | Lead magnet/entry offer, not core subscription |
| Initial gap checklist | High | Low | Low due to free tools | Low | Use for lead qualification |

| Vendor contract tracking only | Medium–high for enterprise SaaS | Medium–high | High with trigger | Medium | Core wedge |

**Retention hypothesis:** teams will pay to avoid repeated document contradictions, customer-deal delays, vendor follow-up work and time spent reconstructing who made a privacy decision. Test this rather than assuming it.

## 4. Exact MVP user story / core loop
"Our team is adding a new analytics tool that receives user email and product events. What do we need to update before release?"

1. Open `New change` and choose `New vendor / New data field / New purpose / Revised notice / Renewal`.
2. Identify one existing or new **processing activity** with data categories, purposes, processing ground **for counsel confirmation**, relevant systems, recipients and owners.
3. Link relevant **vendor**; capture contract version, effective/expiry dates, DPA coverage, required review and evidence references.
4. Compare data map against **notice** version, **vendor** agreement provisions, retention policy and open findings.
5. Deterministic rules generate **review suggestions**, never automatic conclusions of violation. Example: "notice does not mention the newly recorded analytics purpose" or "no signed contract evidence attached".
6. Person reviews highlighted source text, marks accepted/rejected/needs information, assigns owner, due date and review date.
7. Owner uploads (or links to) remediation proof, reviewer approves closure; status becomes `approved` only after reviewer sign-off.
8. Download a concise **readiness change report** recording scope, what changed, evidence, open gaps, actions and exclusions.
9. Monthly check-in: show items due for re-review, unclosed actions, vendor renewals, changed notices; ask 'what changed this month?'.

**Do not build six disconnected tabs. Build ONE traceable chain:**
`ChangeRequest → ProcessingActivity → Vendor / NoticeVersion → Evidence → Finding → Action → ReviewerApproval → Report`.

## 5. MVP feature specification (release order)

### P0 — mandatory for a credible synthetic working prototype
1. Demo sign-in or demo workspace **clearly labelled as fictional/synthetic**; no actual client upload.
2. Overview: open items, owners, reviews due, recent changes. No misleading "98% compliant" score.
3. Processing register: create/read/update/archive activity, categories, purpose, system, recipient, retention description, owner, status. Prevent duplicate IDs, require owner/purpose and show `unverified` provenance.
4. Vendor register: create/read/update/archive vendor, role (processor/other/undetermined), activities served, contractual evidence status, risk-review due, contract renewal, subprocessor notes.
5. Notice version register: title, published date, current status, linked purposes and claimed recipients, comparison notes; no fake auto-scraping.
6. Evidence register: source type, path or controlled upload metadata, file version/date, clause locator, exact snippet, review status. No unsupported citation auto-generation.
7. Findings + remediation: ID, source A, source B or absence, legal reference, severity suggestion, decision status, assigned owner, due date, reviewer and approval. Preserve history.
8. Change requests: new vendor/change processing/add data field/change purpose, actions, approval history.
9. Deterministic checks: absence of evidence, mismatch in linked purposes, unowned item, renewal due, review overdue. All alerts framed as `needs review`.
10. Export CSV/print/PDF-ready report with limitations and source links.

### P1 — before real private pilots
1. Real login and per-organization roles (owner, editor, reviewer, read-only).
2. Tenant isolation with tested database row-level security and protected file storage.
3. Secure file uploads, virus scanning, limits, allowed types, short retention policy, hashing and immutable version metadata.
4. Append-only audit history for changes and approval actions.
5. Domain-verified invite management, rate limiting, request validation, email reminders (opt-in).
6. Backups, restore drills, audit logging, secret management, incident response, documented subprocessor map, security expert review.
7. Mandatory counsel-reviewed rule/citation version and dated effective-date engine.
8. Customer-facing privacy notice, terms, processing contracts, data retention and delete/export flows.

### P2 — after documented demand and operational maturity
- Document extraction: PDF/DOCX text segmentation with page/paragraph references, confidence, human review, no ungrounded claims.
- Monthly change campaign / evidence expiry notifications / scheduled vendor re-review.
- Rights request **metadata** workflow, not raw identifiable data by default.
- Vendor questionnaire upload and negotiation workflow.
- Controlled AI assistance for extraction only, provider contract/config review first.

### Explicitly OUT of scope for MVP
- A registered Consent Manager, real consent collection, browser SDK and cookie banner.
- Automated data discovery across Google Workspace, AWS, databases and CRMs.
- Automatic DPDP certification, legal opinion, SDF declaration, penalty prediction or legal advice.
- Always-on live breach response without specialist supervision.
- Universal DPO/DPIA mandate, real-time user profiling, full DSAR identity processing.
- Bidirectional enterprise integrations, complex workflow builders, payment SaaS metering, RAG chat-first design.

## 7. Data model (Postgres, designed for tenant isolation)
All tenant-owned tables have: `id UUID primary key`, `organization_id UUID not null`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `created_by UUID`, `archived_at nullable`. Prefer **one database, one consistent organization_id**, not a separate database per company for the first pilots.

| Table | Essential columns | Relations / constraints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| organizations | name, legal_entity, retention_policy_version | root tenant |
| memberships | organization_id, user_id, role, invited_at, revoked_at | unique org+user; role allowlist |
| processing_activities | activity_code, title, purpose, asserted_ground, ground_review_status, source_system, recipient_description, data_categories jsonb, retention_rule, owner_user_id, review_due_at | unique org+activity_code |
| vendors | name, role_classification, contact_ref, contract_status, renewal_at, subprocessor_notes, owner_user_id, review_due_at | unique org+normalized name |
| activity_vendors | activity_id, vendor_id, data_category_summary | unique pair, org-consistent FK validation |
| notice_versions | name, version, effective_at, status, purpose_summary, recipient_summary, evidence_id | only one current version per notice type/organization |
| evidences | file_ref, filename, mime_type, sha256, version, reference_locator, exact_excerpt, upload_review_status, retained_until | private storage, no public URLs |
| change_requests | title, change_type, description, activity_id, vendor_id, initiated_by, owner_user_id, state, review_due_at | draft → review → approved/rejected |
| findings | rule_id, title, description, origin, source_a_evidence_id, source_b_evidence_id, absence_note, legal_reference_id, severity_proposal, reviewer_severity, state, owner_user_id, due_at | no `confirmed` without reviewer; `absence_note` allowed |
| remediation_actions | finding_id, title, assignee_user_id, state, due_at, closure_evidence_id, approved_by, approved_at | closure requires evidence + approver |
| legal_references | official_title, section, rule, effective_at, text_excerpt, official_url, version, verified_by, verified_at | centrally maintained, non-tenant or override |
| audit_events | organization_id, actor_user_id, entity_type, entity_id, verb, before_hash, after_hash, event_at | append-only to application users |

Use a consistent reference identifier `ACT-001`, `VEN-001`, `FND-001`, but UUID under the hood. For sensitive text, avoid storing raw end-user personal data. User-settable 'legal ground' remains **tentative until validated**; do not auto-classify consent vs legitimate use.

## 8. Trust and deployment architecture
`Next.js + TypeScript + Tailwind + accessible component system → server-side actions/route handlers with auth+authorization → Supabase Auth + Postgres RLS → private Supabase Storage → reporting/export`.

For V0 zero-cost synthetic demo: self-contained HTML or a static React app, seeded data only and no user accounts. For P1 pilot: Next.js App Router, Supabase Auth and private Postgres. Avoid duplicative FastAPI, Kubernetes, queues, vector DB and integrations at the beginning. Email can be manually sent until a pattern of recurring reminders is proven.

Security boundaries:
- RLS enabled and **tested** for every exposed tenant table and storage object. Restrict SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE independently and deny cross-org access. Never trust `organization_id` passed by clients.
- AuthN ≠ AuthZ: validate membership and role for every server action and route; log sensitive events.
- Do not ship a service-role secret in browser code or logs. Never use a bypass-RLS connection for normal customer requests.
- Explicit read/write tests with two organizations, including foreign ID tampering, nested relations and storage objects.
- Request validation with Zod; limits on payload sizes and uploads; rate limits; safe error text; clear separation of demo seed vs production data.
- Enforce private storage, expiring access links, file-type allowlist, malware checks, report filtering and retention/delete policy.
- Secrets in environment, never UI. HTTPS and platform-level security settings. Backups and recovery test before paid production usage.
- Acquire independent technical security review before storing real client agreements/data. A working demo is **not production-ready merely because code compiles**.

Authoritative implementation references:

- https://supabase.com/kb/faqs/database/multi-tenant-isolation
- https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/authentication

## 9. Rule engine (start deterministic, zero AI API cost)
All rule outputs are `needs_review`, not `noncompliant`.

| Rule | Inputs | Suggested action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MAP_OWNERSHIP_MISSING | processing activity without owner | Assign accountable reviewer |
| VENDOR_CONTRACT_EVIDENCE_MISSING | vendor used in activity + no valid-contract evidence | Request contract or explain unavailable evidence |
| VENDOR_RENEWAL_DUE | contract expiration within configurable days | Re-review vendor agreement |
| NOTICE_PURPOSE_MISMATCH | activity purpose absent from notice-purpose mapping | Human review notice and processing ground |
| NOTICE_RECIPIENT_MISMATCH | activity linked vendor not reflected in claims, where applicable | Check actual notice wording and necessity of correction |
| RETENTION_RULE_UNDEFINED | processing activity with blank retention rule | Establish documented retention disposition |
| REMEDIATION_OVERDUE | due date past and not closed | Escalate to owner |
| EVIDENCE_NOT_VERIFIED | assessment references unverified evidence | Stop final sign-off |
| REVIEW_OVERDUE | vendor/activity review date past | Schedule re-review |

Avoid auto-generating legally decisive findings when a data field is absent; show "not established from available evidence". Duplicate suppress with stable fingerprint `(organization_id, rule_id, subject_entity_id, evidence_version)`.

## 10. Legal knowledge engine
Separate `law` from `business policies` and `proposed contract positions`:
- `STATUTE` e.g. DPDP Act s.8(2), source Official Gazette, enacted text, phased effective date.
- `RULE` e.g. DPDP Rule 3 or Rule 6, source Official Gazette, phased effective date.
- `BUSINESS_CONTROL` e.g. quarterly vendor review, **not necessarily expressly required by DPDP**.
- `NEGOTIATION` e.g. 12-hour supplier alert, no claim of legal deadline.
- `INTERPRETATION` counsel-reviewed point with citation, reviewer, timestamp.

Every legal assertion in a generated report must carry exact source, provision, effective date and verified version. When uncertain, mark `requires legal review` and omit it from automatic compliance determinations. Never claim universal 'compliant' status.

## 11. Core onboarding and reporting workflow
**Intake**: 20-minute kickoff, company scope, decision owner, major products, current notice URL/doc, top five processors, key purpose/field list, existing vendor contracts where permitted. Redact personal identifiers and restrict real uploads until pilot security review.

**Processing records**: map top three flows first: (1) signup/identity; (2) product use/support; (3) sales/CRM. Record exact data categories and source-to-vendor relationships.

**Vendor review**: start with the five vendors that receive most personal data or touch customer deals. Record agreement status and missing proof, not invented vendor vulnerabilities.

**Change review**: buyer says "we introduced a new messaging tool". Document change, map vendor, check notice/purpose and contract, assign actions, get reviewer approval.

**Monthly retainer**: check change inbox; chase two open vendor DPAs; close three findings with proof; perform review of notice changes; send one-page change digest, owners and risks. Agree maximum vendor/action allowance contractually.

**Reports** follow original file sequence: scope/documents, not reviewed, counts and top findings, findings table, contradictions, statutory ceiling context only where justified, 30/60/90 plan, annexes, limitations/sign-off.

- **Tier 1:** ₹35k pilot Notice & Vendor Review, 5 working days (later ₹45–60k only if paid conversions support it).
- **Tier 2:** ₹75k–₹1.25L Readiness Sprint, 10 working days (credit Tier 1 on upgrade as contracted).

## 13. 8-week execution with gating

**Week 0 — Customer discovery (not code):** take original uploaded skill files as operating manuals, define the product job, assemble 20 targeted companies, run 8–12 interviews. Evidence sought: at least 3–5 describe the same painful event (a vendor change, missing owner, stale notice), at least two agree to a paid scope. No sales guarantee.

**Week 1 — Synthetic prototype:** implement all screens and full change-to-approval loop in a self-contained demo (the bundled HTML proves presentation only). Use fictional company and no real client documents. Show walkthrough to target buyers.

**Week 2 — Paid review pilot:** sign scoped Tier 1/Tier 2 engagement; deliver manually using source skills and counsel/specialist support; record step times and exceptions. Do not send private docs to the synthetic HTML demo.

**Weeks 3–4 — Product design and validation:** extract repeatable recurring fields from paid reviews; define the data model and access matrix on paper. **Only begin a real database-backed internal app after the source skill’s five-paid-deliveries gate**; otherwise continue manual delivery and refine the synthetic demo.

**Weeks 5–6 — Operational loops (conditional on paid evidence):** if five paid deliveries have confirmed repeated steps, implement the real database-backed processing register, vendor register, notice versions, evidence and findings, change request, export and action approvals; run tenant tests. Otherwise keep selling and servicing manually.

**Weeks 7–8 — Controlled pilot (not automatic):** move to 1–2 explicitly consented design partners **only if** paid-use evidence and legal/privacy/security release gates have been met. Exercise backup, delete, access revocation, cross-tenant tests and restore drill. If gates are not met, continue synthetic demos and secure manual work instead.

**Beyond 8 weeks:** only build client self-service when 5 paid deliveries show repeated process; source skill suggests full evidence room after ~10 paid sprints. Prioritize commercially validated friction over speculative product breadth.

## 14. Acceptance tests / definition of done
### Functional demo
- User creates an activity, a vendor, links them, creates change request, sees a deterministic "needs review" item, assigns an action, provides closure evidence note, reviewer approves, exports a report.
- Change events update due lists and cannot silently overwrite original evidence versions.
- Missing evidence never becomes a "proven violation"; completion requires reviewer.
- Every displayed user-facing statute claim shows an official source + effective date or is labelled `needs legal verification`.

### Customer pilot release gates
- 100% tenant-owned tables and storage paths covered by RLS/authorization tests; cross-tenant reads/writes/files denied.
- Authentication and authorization checked on every mutation; role revocation works.
- No public files, leaked secrets, raw principal identifiers in demo; upload limits; malware controls; backups and recovery verified.
- Review and approval trail cannot be modified by ordinary editors.
- Actual statutory references reviewed against current official Gazette and recorded as verified.
- A qualified security professional has assessed real-data risks; engagement and data-processing terms in place.
- No false claims to be Board-registered, accredited, compliant, a DPO, or an advocate.

## 15. Commercial validation dashboard (manual Sheet initially)
Weekly: contacts → qualified replies → calls → proposals → paid sprints; average days to first payment; hours to produce evidence; number of review changes; top recurring exception; documents delayed; actual service gross margin; 90-day retainer conversion; customers who use software more than 2 times monthly; monthly actions closed and report opened. Mark sample size and don't mistake pilot feedback for universal demand.

### Hard stop/pivot rules
- If 15–20 qualified conversations produce no budget owner or meaningful recurring incident, narrow the ICP/change event, not the feature list.
- If buyers only want one-time reports, sell Tier 1/2 and do not force SaaS.
- If two paid clients demand the same repeat workflow, automate **that** step.
- If a security review fails, don't expose real client data; keep running synthetic demo and manual confidential delivery.
- If tool cost or service hours eliminate contribution margin, simplify scope or increase minimum price after testing.
